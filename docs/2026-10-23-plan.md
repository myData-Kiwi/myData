## 1. What went wrong

1. **The phones were scheduled last, so you can't use any of it.**
   - The host refuses any address that isn't loopback (`host/src/mydata/service/config.py:240`).
   - CardDAV/CalDAV serving is phase 9 of the build order and remote access is phase 10.
   - Your contacts, calendar and mail live on your iPhone and Samsung, and nothing myData holds reaches them. Contacts you create are labelled "On myData only, not on your phone yet".
   - Owning data your devices can't see is worse than today, because it forks your address book.
2. **Edge cases came before the basics.**
   - `ical_edit.py` has 1,265 lines and about 10 fix commits for daylight-saving splits and Outlook zone names, and no route or screen uses it.
   - Contact import has zip-bomb caps, UTF-16 decoding, a per-card Windows-1252 guess and eight shadowing rules (H1–H8), for something you do once.
   - Meanwhile there is no installer, no login item, no remote access and no way to create an event.
3. **"Done" meant "passes against fakes".**
   - Phase 4 is marked done while "the apps have not run against a live host", and you have never had a session using the apps.
   - 81 of the 288 commits are fixes (28%), against 98 features. Most came from spec, review and fix loops, not from use.
4. **The product shape is the most expensive one available.** Four separate apps, going on six or more, mean:
   - four runners, four menu bars and four things to sign;
   - scopes per app and links between apps;
   - the rule that an app replaces the host on connect.

   Shared code doesn't explain the split: myAssistant's own `lib` is 344 lines. (Free iPhone signing is not the argument here, because no native iPhone app is planned. Maintenance cost and how it feels to use are.)
5. **The design system was built ahead of the product.**
   - `mydata_ui` has 24,059 hand-written lines, more than the host's 22,482.
   - `ColourPicker`, `TimePicker` and `TokenField` (about 2,400 lines) are used by no app, yet each has a spec, a preview and goldens.
   - 372 golden PNGs and about 134,000 words of documents buy about seven visible features.
6. **"It doesn't look good" has one main cause.** `design/platforms.md` makes "colour, type, shape, cards" ours everywhere, so the apps draw their own chrome on every platform. The result:
   - mobile density on the Mac (15 px body, 44 px controls, 56 px rows, about 9 contacts in an 800 px window);
   - lists made of cards on a grey ground;
   - fonts that aren't the system's (Figtree, Plus Jakarta Sans), Material icons and a red focus ring;
   - no toolbar and no translucent sidebar;
   - the default Flutter icon on all four apps (`app_icon_256.png` is byte-identical across them).
7. **The host is a dev checkout on a fanless laptop.**
   - There is no bundled host and no login item, and builds are signed ad hoc.
   - Every rebuild asks for Contacts and Calendars again; on 14 Oct a rebuild emptied the This Mac copy.
   - Quitting myData stops the host, and closing the lid stops it too.
8. **The assistant is a search box.**
   - In real mode it has two read-only tools, and chained requests pass 26% of the time.
   - The model is about a year old and there is no index across sources.
   - It lacks the base (index, timeline, extraction) that would make a private assistant worth more than Siri or Gemini.
9. **The work isn't backed up.** 211 commits exist only on this laptop and there is no CI. All 288 commits fall between 11 and 14 Oct, and nothing has been committed in the 9 days since.
10. **CLAUDE.md is now a cost.** At 12,133 words, every session pays to read history that belongs in `docs/decisions/`.

None of this makes the code bad. The safety net, the lossless vCard editor, the permission gate and the token scopes are sound. What was wrong was the order of the work and the definition of done.

## 2. The objective and the product model

**The objective.** myData is your own iCloud plus a private brain, on hardware you own.
- The host stores your contacts, calendars, tasks, mail archive, photos and files.
- It serves them to every device through standard protocols, so the phones' own apps just work.
- It keeps every version, never loses anything, and runs local AI over all of it, with nothing sent to a model provider.
- Apple, Google, Samsung and Microsoft each see only their own slice; myData sees all of it, for one household.
- It stays non-commercial and open source.

**Who it's for.** First you (a Mac, an iPhone and a Samsung, with data spread across iCloud, Google and Microsoft), then your household, then other self-hosters.

**What "done" looks like for you:**
- **Contacts:** you add a contact on the iPhone and it is on the Samsung and the Mac the next time those devices sync (see section 4 for real sync times). iCloud and Google are no longer where your contacts live.
- **Calendars:** your personal events live in the myData calendar and ring on both phones. Shared and work calendars stay with their providers and stay visible (followed), until invitations exist (M8).
- **The app:** opening myData gives you one search across everything, today's brief, each person's page, and an assistant that answers from your own data and cites its sources.
- **Running it:** nothing needs a terminal, nothing asks for permission twice, and the host is back after a reboot.
- **Safety:** there is always a verified copy of your data on a disk other than the host's.
- **Looks:** it looks as if it belongs on each device.

**The principles:**

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| Standards first, our UI second | Where a protocol exists (CardDAV, CalDAV, IMAP, WebDAV), the platform's own apps are the clients. Our UI covers what no platform app does. |
| Minimal surface, power under Settings | Main screens show content and three or four actions. Power features sit behind Settings, menus or ⌘K. One sentence per state; details behind ⓘ. |
| Everything big companies offer, over time | Three routes: standards (the phones' apps bring widgets, Siri and alerts), integration (Immich, a Bitwarden-compatible server), and a shared base (one index) so each new feature is a view, not a project. |
| Private local AI is the differentiator | Works across vendors, on an archive that is never purged, with a source for every fact and the gate on every irreversible act. Nothing leaves the house, and the host can list the connections it made. |
| Platform owns the frame, myData owns the content | Window, toolbar, sidebar, controls, font, icons and accent are the system's. The kiwi, avatars, person pages, brief, empty states and wording are ours. |
| Non-commercial | No paid service on the main path. One-off hardware and an optional Apple fee are your choice, never a requirement. |
| Done means used | A milestone is done when you have used it for three days on real data, not when its tests pass. |
| No single copy | Before any provider copy is switched off, the host's data has a verified copy off the machine. |

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| The M4 Air (now) | **Acceptable for M1, as a stopgap, behind the backup gate.** The phones keep full local copies, so they work while the laptop sleeps and sync when it wakes. The cost: no mail fetching, brief or indexing with the lid shut, and it is also your development machine. |
| **Mac mini, Apple silicon, recommended household host** | Always on, about 3 W idle ([ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/productfinder/product/certified-computers/details/4513879)). 24 GB is enough for qwen3:14b-class models; **32 GB or more** if larger models (Gemma 4 26B class) are a goal. **Recommendation:** the Air now, a Mac mini after M3 if you use it daily. Keep the core host platform-neutral, with This Mac and Mail.app as optional connectors.

**Remote access: Tailscale (recommended), with its own security design (section 4).**
- **Cost and reach:** the free Personal plan covers up to 6 users with unlimited devices, for non-commercial use, which fits ([pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing/)).
- **HTTPS:** Tailscale Serve gives HTTPS on a `*.ts.net` name, reachable only inside your tailnet ([Serve](https://tailscale.com/kb/1312/serve)). Never Funnel.
- **Trade-offs:** a third-party coordination account, and machine names appear in public certificate logs ([Tailscale HTTPS](https://tailscale.com/kb/1153/enabling-https/)).
- **Fallback:** plain WireGuard, under Advanced.
- **Rejected:** Cloudflare Tunnel, because Cloudflare terminates TLS and sees the traffic (its terms are from secondary sources, unverified).
- **Unverified:** iOS CardDAV/CalDAV over a `ts.net` certificate, and the battery cost of the Tailscale VPN on the iPhone. Both are tested on day 1 of M1b.

## 4. Architecture after these decisions

| Layer | Default |
|---|---|
| **Host process** | Python and FastAPI, kept. Shipped as a signed `.app` with a bundled interpreter (sqlite-vec, icalendar and the nested helper all signed), started by a LaunchAgent at login and independent of every app. **About one week of work, budgeted in M1a.** Installed on your Macs by a local script, because an Apple Development-signed app downloaded from GitHub is quarantined and blocked by Gatekeeper without Developer ID and notarisation. Power assertions only on mains with the lid open (option). Linux container later. |
| **Data** | One SQLite file per service. An `items` table in M3 (id, kind, source, time, people, title, text, provenance), filled by every store, with FTS5 and sqlite-vec and a purge that cascades to the index. **From M1b, every collection has an owner person and every device credential maps to a person**, so a partner's paired phone never receives your address book. The household UI comes later. |
| **Backups** (new row) | Local snapshots as now, **plus** an encrypted, versioned copy on an external disk you own (Time Machine with the live `*.sqlite*` excluded, or restic over the snapshots). `mydata backup verify` runs against the **external** copy. `/status` shows "last off-machine backup: N days ago", red after 7 days. A written restore runbook, tried once on a fresh `MYDATA_HOME`. A second disk kept away from home and rotated monthly is recommended. **This is the M1b gate before iCloud Contacts goes off, and before passwords are ever stored.** |
| **Remote listener** (new row) | A **second listener**, the only thing Tailscale Serve proxies to. It accepts only per-device credentials (`dav`, `web`). App tokens, admin, MCP, approve, import, shutdown and the `mydata` all-scope token never reach it. Device credentials are bound to a person and checked against Serve's `Tailscale-User-Login` header (present only from untagged devices; [Serve](https://tailscale.com/kb/1312/serve)), as defence in depth. DAV Basic auth gets rate limits and a lockout. Tailscale ACLs allow only your devices, with two-factor sign-in on the identity provider and Tailnet Lock (free-plan availability unverified). Settings, Devices shows last seen and revoke, and a "lost phone" path. Never Funnel. The loopback API stays as it is. |
| **Sync to phones** | The host serves **CardDAV and CalDAV** (events, and VTODO as plain tasks) with sync-collection (RFC 6578) and etags. Clients: iPhone (Settings, Add Account, Other, or a generated `.mobileconfig`; payload fields unverified), Samsung through DAVx5 (free on F-Droid: [DAVx5](https://www.davx5.com/download)), and the Mac's Contacts, Calendar and Reminders. **Build:** day 1 runs stock Radicale against all three clients and records their traffic; our own server is then built against those recordings and replayed as tests. A Radicale storage plugin runs inside Radicale's process and is GPL-bound, so it is an option only if the licence decision allows it. |
| **Sync timing (realistic)** | Phone to host: under 1 minute. Host to iPhone: no push exists for third-party DAV servers. iOS fetches on a schedule (reported as about hourly for CardDAV) and when Calendar is opened ([Nextcloud forum](https://help.nextcloud.com/t/ios-not-syncing-with-nextcloud-caldav-in-background/132476); [CalConnect PUSH](https://www.calconnect.org/about/technical-committees/tc-push/)). Fastmail's iOS push relies on an Apple mechanism not available to us (how they got it is unverified; [Fastmail](https://www.fastmail.com/blog/push-sync-for-contacts-calendars-now-available-on-ios/)). Host to Samsung: DAVx5 polls with Android's 15-minute minimum, unless the server implements WebDAV-Push ([DAVx5 manual](https://manual.davx5.com/webdav_push.html)). **Later:** WebDAV-Push to DAVx5 through UnifiedPush once ntfy exists. |
| **Samsung** | One UI puts DAVx5, Tailscale and ntfy to sleep after about 3 days unused, and its settings can revert after updates ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/samsung)). Pairing includes a checklist (battery unrestricted, never sleeping), and the host flags a device that hasn't synced in 24 hours. |
| **Sync to our own app** | The JMAP model: `GET /sync/{collection}?since=<token>` returns changes and deletions; writes use If-Match (built); one SSE channel says only "something changed". The app keeps a drift (SQLite) cache and an outbox; a 412 opens the existing Reload or keep-mine choice. No CRDTs, no Postgres sync products ([Electric writes](https://electric.ax/docs/guides/writes)). |
| **Offline** | Phones: full DAV copies. The app: drift cache, read offline, writes queued. The phone web page: a service-worker cache of Today and recent results. |
| **Notifications** | Calendar alerts ring through the phones' calendar apps (VALARM), which ends the "alerts stay hidden" default for events in the myData calendar. myData's own notices: Android through UnifiedPush with a self-hosted ntfy on the tailnet ([ntfy](https://docs.ntfy.sh/config/)); iPhone through Web Push to the Home Screen page; the Mac through local notifications. APNs only if you pay $99. |
| **Installers and updates** | Mac: the local install script on your machines. Developer ID, notarisation and Sparkle only for a public release ($99). Android: an APK from GitHub Releases, updated with Obtainium. iPhone: the web page needs no install. `make release` builds and tags all of them. |
| **AI** | The model worker is its own process, reachable over the network so it can move to a Mac mini. **On the Air:** a bake-off of qwen3:14b against qwen3.5:9b (6.6 GB), pinned to an Ollama version without the bug where qwen3.5 prints tool calls as text ([ollama#14745](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14745)), on a **new** frozen set, measuring memory with the host, the app and an embedding model loaded. **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** is about 18 GB as `gemma4:26b` (secondary sources: [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/?p=331053), [issoh](https://www.issoh.co.jp/tech/details/11651/); unverified). A model of about 17 GB already crashed this 24 GB Air, so it is tested only on a 32 GB or larger Mac mini. Embeddings: EmbeddingGemma 308M or Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, chosen on a small synthetic retrieval set (unverified). Speech later: Parakeet, or Whisper if recordings are in Gujarati or Hindi (your call). |
| **AI safety rules** (in CLAUDE.md before any extraction ships) | (1) A session that has read mail, invite or web text loses outbound tools unless each call is approved ([lethal trifecta](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). (2) Extraction jobs have no tools, only typed output. (3) Every extracted fact links to its source and is shown as a suggestion. (4) The assistant answers only from what it retrieved, or says it found nothing. (5) Calendar and mail text are untrusted; hidden instructions in invites have already been used against Gemini ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-gemini-indirect-prompt-injection-attack/)). |

## 5. Design direction

### Visual principles

1. **Density per platform.**

   | Platform | Body | Controls | Rows |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | macOS | SF Pro 13 pt | 28 pt default, 20 pt minimum | 24 to 32 pt |
   | iPhone (web page) | 17 pt | 44 pt targets | |
   | Android | 16 sp | 48 dp targets | |

   Sources: [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). The single `control-height: 44` and `row-height: 56` tokens go.
2. **Lists are lists.** One continuous source list with thin separators and one accent selection fill. Inset groups only in forms and Settings.
3. **The system's chrome.** Full-size content view, transparent title bar, translucent sidebar ([macos_window_utils](https://pub.dev/documentation/macos_window_utils/latest/)), and a real toolbar with search, New and the view switch. Settings in its own window (⌘,). No app name typed in the sidebar.
4. **The system's accent and focus ring.** Kiwi green is an accent option. One tinted primary action per screen.
5. **Show, don't explain.** One line of secondary text per state. Ownership and source as small glyph badges. Explanations behind ⓘ, and the loss report folds to a count.
6. **Brand where it counts.** A layered app icon in Icon Composer from the existing kiwi SVG (light, dark, tinted), the kiwi on first launch and in empty states, avatars and person headers.
7. **Motion.** Springs: SwiftUI-like smooth and snappy, and Material's standard scheme. The card stagger goes. Reduced motion becomes a cross-fade.
8. **Status only when something is wrong.** A menu-bar extra shows the host's state (Open, Quit host). A window banner appears only when the host can't be reached, the off-machine backup is stale, or a device hasn't synced ([HIG Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)).

### Changes in `design/` and `mydata_ui` (if Flutter wins the Mac)

- Rewrite `platforms.md`; `tokens.json` gets `density.{macos,ios,android}`; `themes.json` becomes accent sets.
- Fonts: the system font on Apple platforms, Roboto or Google Sans on Android. Figtree and Plus Jakarta Sans leave the apps; the website keeps its own.
- An icon adapter: SF Symbols (or Lucide) on Apple, Material Symbols on Android.
- New widgets: `SourceList`, `Toolbar`, `SettingsWindow`, `CommandBar`.
- Restyled: Dialog (push buttons, the default button in the accent), Inspector, and toasts (on the Mac undo is ⌘Z; toasts only on phones).
- Move to `material_ui`/`cupertino_ui`. Official docs: opt in from Flutter 3.47, deprecation of the in-SDK libraries "in an upcoming stable" release ([Flutter docs](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/material-ui-and-cupertino-ui)).

If SwiftUI wins the Mac, `mydata_ui` keeps serving Android only, and the Mac uses AppKit/SwiftUI's own controls.

### Decided while building the M1a look pass (2026-10-23; the owner may revisit)

- **Toasts stay on the Mac for now.** The plan says "on the Mac undo is ⌘Z; toasts only on phones". ⌘Z and Edit > Undo (named from the action) work in every app, but the toast is today the only thing on screen that says a delete went to Recently Deleted and offers Undo there. It is drawn as a small Mac panel. Revisit once the Edit menu's undo name is seen in use.
- **Settings stays a section of myData's window, opened with ⌘,** (Settings… in the app menu), with its tabs in the toolbar. A separate Settings window needs Flutter's multi-window support, which is still experimental on macOS in Flutter 3.47. The host-wide theme row is named "Theme on other devices", since the Mac uses the system's accent; an accent choice for the Mac comes with the Settings window.
- **No view switch in myCalendar until a second view exists** (Apple ships no disabled view modes), and today is marked in the system red, as Calendar.app does; the selected day has a thin accent outline.
- **myAssistant has no sidebar** until conversation history exists: with one conversation it would be one row repeating the title.
- **App icons:** each app its own colour and one white glyph (Dusk, Rose and Plum from the themes; myData stays the pale green kiwi), the lime of the kiwi's wash as the shared detail, an Icon Composer `.icon` with dark variants for macOS 26 beside the classic icon (`design/assets/Logos/app-icons`).

## 7. How the work will run differently

1. **Live use first.** Every milestone runs on your real devices against the real host. Fakes are for unit tests, never for "done".
2. **Only the next milestone is planned in detail.** Detailed commitments cover M0 to M1c. Everything after is a sketch, revised after each milestone. No spec before first use.
3. **Your time is budgeted.** Each milestone lists the sessions it needs from you. DAV compatibility is debugged with your phones in hand, so that is the pace-setter.
4. **Visual bar.** Each screen is checked against Apple's (or Samsung's) equivalent, with two screenshots at the same size, and you sign off. Goldens catch regressions; they aren't the bar.
5. **Tests where data can be lost.** The host keeps its full safety tests (migrations, triggers, undo, purge, and DAV writes against the recorded client traffic). Driving live clients (macOS Contacts, DAVx5 in an emulator) is automated as far as it goes (unverified how far). No new tests for unused components.
6. **Checkable acceptance.** Every criterion you can't judge by eye gets a command or a debug readout (`mydata check security`, `mydata bench report`, query times, kept and dismissed counts).
7. **Release.** Each milestone ends with `make release`: the Mac app installed by the local script, an APK, and the web page, tagged on GitHub.
8. **The work is backed up.** Push at the end of every session; CI runs on every push.
9. **Steady pace.** A check-in at the end of each week on what you used and what annoyed you. Annoyances go ahead of new features.
10. **Edge cases need evidence.** A daylight-saving or charset case is fixed when real data hits it.
